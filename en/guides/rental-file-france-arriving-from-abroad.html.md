# How do I put together a French rental file when I arrive from abroad?

> When you arrive from abroad, your French rental file, the “dossier de location”, holds the same four kinds of documents as any other applicant’s, using the papers you have. A passport or a residence permit serves as your identity document. An employment contract, or a letter from your employer, proves your occupation. If your income was taxed in another country, [décret n° 2015-1437](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493) provides for that country’s tax notice. The documents must be written in French or translated into French, with amounts converted into euros.

## Which identity document should you provide?

One valid document showing your photo: a passport, an identity card or a driving licence, French or foreign, or a document proving your right to stay in France, such as a residence permit or a resident card. This is the [decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493)’s list.

## What can you provide without a French tax notice?

The [decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493) covers this: when all or part of your income was taxed in another country, the document is that country’s tax notice, or an equivalent document from its tax authority. You can also prove your income with other documents on the list, for example a grant award notice.

## What can you provide without French payslips?

The [decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493) lists the “last three payslips” without naming a country. Our reading: foreign payslips, translated, can be presented. If you are starting a job, the employment or internship contract is on the list of documents about your occupation. Failing that, a letter from the employer states the job, the proposed pay, the start date and, if there is one, the length of the trial period. A student provides a student card or a certificate of enrolment for the current year.

## Do you have to translate your documents?

Yes. The [decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493) requires documents written in French or translated into French, with amounts converted into euros. It does not say who must translate. [DossierFacile](https://www.dossierfacile.logement.gouv.fr/blog/etudiant-etranger-logement), the government service, writes in its blog that it accepts sworn translations, and that you can also translate the key points yourself, directly on the document. That flexibility is DossierFacile’s: a landlord may ask for more.

## What if you have no guarantor in France?

[Visale](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F33453), the free guarantee from Action Logement, can stand in for a guarantor. You apply before signing the lease. According to [Campus France](https://www.campusfrance.org/en/VISALE-free-rental-deposit-for-students), a student aged 18 to 30 who needs a visa to come to France must have obtained a long-stay visa that serves as a residence permit, marked “étudiant” or “passeport talent”, and can start the application as soon as that visa is issued. The other options are in the guide on [renting without a guarantor](https://monsieurdossier.com/en/guides/rent-in-france-without-guarantor.html).

## Where should you start?

1. Gather one identity document, one document about your occupation and one about your income.
2. Translate into French anything that is not, and convert the amounts into euros.
3. Apply for the Visale visa if you qualify: you show it to the landlord with your file.
4. Create a file on DossierFacile if the landlord accepts its link. In [its blog](https://www.dossierfacile.logement.gouv.fr/blog/etudiant-etranger-logement), the service names the passport or the residence permit as the identity document.
5. Pay nothing before the lease is signed: according to [service-public.gouv.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F1169), a landlord has no right to ask.
6. Protect the copies you send by email: see [what the watermark should say](https://monsieurdossier.com/en/guides/watermark-text-rental-documents-france.html).

> For a stay of one to ten months during higher education, an internship, an apprenticeship, vocational training, civic service, a job transfer or a temporary assignment, the [“bail mobilité” lease](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F34759) is an option: the home is furnished and, under the [law](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000509310/), the landlord cannot ask for a security deposit.
