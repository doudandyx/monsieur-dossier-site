# Can I rent a home in France without a guarantor?

> Yes, you can rent in France without a guarantor. The law does not require a tenant to have one: it is the landlord who chooses to ask. If nobody close to you can act as guarantor, [Visale](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F33453), a guarantee from Action Logement, can stand in for one. It is free and open to people aged up to 30, to some older employees and to tenants on a “bail mobilité” lease. You have to apply before you sign the lease.

## Can a landlord insist on a guarantor?

Yes, the [law of 6 July 1989](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000509310/) allows it, within limits set in its articles 22-1 and 22-2.

- A landlord who has taken out insurance against unpaid rent cannot ask for a guarantor as well, unless the tenant is a student or an apprentice.
- A landlord cannot refuse a guarantor on the ground that the person is not French or does not live in mainland France.
- A landlord cannot require an ascendant or a descendant of the applicant, a parent or a child for example, to co-sign the lease.
- When the landlord is a company, other than a family property company, it can ask for a guarantor only if the guarantee comes from a body on a list set by decree, or if the home is let to a student who does not receive a higher-education grant.

> In practice, [DossierFacile](https://www.dossierfacile.logement.gouv.fr/blog/etudiant-etranger-logement), the government service, writes that some landlords accept a guarantor living abroad, but that this remains rare.

## What is the Visale guarantee?

Visale is a free guarantee granted by [Action Logement](https://www.actionlogement.fr/la-garantie-visale). If rent goes unpaid or the home is damaged, Action Logement first pays the landlord the sum owed, then gets it back from the tenant.

According to [service-public.gouv.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F33453), it covers at most 36 months of unpaid rent during the first three years of the lease, and damage up to two months of rent and charges. You apply online, before signing the lease. You receive a “visa” to hand to the landlord. For the guarantee to apply, the landlord must then accept the guarantee deed.

## Who can get Visale?

According to [service-public.gouv.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F33453) and [Action Logement](https://www.actionlogement.fr/la-garantie-visale), read on 24 November 2026:

- people aged 30 and under, whatever their work situation, students and work-study trainees included
- older private-sector employees, if their net pay does not exceed €1,710 a month, or if they have just been hired or transferred and apply within six months
- tenants who sign a “bail mobilité” lease, whatever their age

The rent, charges included, is capped: €1,940 in the Paris region, €1,575 or €1,365 elsewhere depending on the town. An income condition applies depending on your situation, and lower caps apply to students and work-study trainees who cannot show proof of income. The official eligibility test, on [service-public.gouv.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F33453), gives the answer for your case.

## Is Visale open to international students?

Yes, according to [Campus France](https://www.campusfrance.org/en/VISALE-free-rental-deposit-for-students): you only need to be between 18 and 30. A student who needs a visa to come to France must have obtained a long-stay visa that serves as a residence permit, marked “étudiant” or “passeport talent”. A student who is a citizen of a European Union country shows a student card and a valid passport. Campus France recommends starting the application as soon as the visa is issued.

## What are the other options without a guarantor?

- **A bank guarantee.** According to [DossierFacile](https://www.dossierfacile.logement.gouv.fr/blog/etudiant-etranger-logement), you can deposit a sum with a French bank, which serves as security if rent goes unpaid.
- **A legal entity.** According to [service-public.gouv.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F31267), the guarantor can be a person, such as a parent or a friend, or a legal entity: Action Logement, a company, a bank.
- **The “bail mobilité” lease.** Under the [law](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000509310/), a lease of one to ten months for a furnished home, reserved for people in higher education, on an internship, in an apprenticeship, in vocational training, in civic service, on a job transfer or on a temporary assignment. The landlord cannot ask for a security deposit.

## What money can a landlord ask for?

No sum before the lease is signed, according to [service-public.gouv.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F1169). After that, the [law](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000509310/) caps the security deposit at one month of rent excluding charges for an unfurnished home and two months for a furnished one, and forbids it for a “bail mobilité”. If rent is payable more than two months in advance, no security deposit is allowed.

## Which documents can a guarantor be asked for?

According to [décret n° 2015-1437](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493): one identity document, one proof of address, and documents about occupation and income. The list is in the guide on [the documents a landlord can ask for](https://monsieurdossier.com/en/guides/documents-landlord-can-ask-france.html).
