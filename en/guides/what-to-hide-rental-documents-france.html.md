# What can I hide on my French rental documents?

> No law sets out what an applicant can hide on French rental documents. Our reading: you can hide what serves neither to assess your file nor to verify the document, such as your IBAN, your social security number or the line of codes at the bottom of a passport. Leave visible your tax number, the tax notice reference and the 2D-Doc code, which are used to verify the notice, plus your photo, name and income. As far as we know, nothing obliges a landlord to accept a redacted copy, and [décret n° 2015-1437](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493) lets them ask for the original.

## What does a landlord not need to see?

This is our reading, not a written rule. These three items serve neither to assess your ability to pay nor to verify the document:

- **Your IBAN and bank details (RIB)**, which a payslip sometimes shows. The RIB is not on the [decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493)’s list.
- **Your social security number**, which a payslip may contain. About a different case, the [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-11/logement_social-fiche04.pdf) counts it among the payslip data that are in principle not needed.
- **The line of codes at the bottom of a passport or identity card**, called the MRZ, which repeats your details in a machine-readable form.

> For a social housing application the rules are different: according to the [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-11/logement_social-fiche04.pdf), the social security number of the adults who will live in the home is part of the information requested.

## What must stay visible?

- **Your tax number and the tax notice reference.** According to [impots.gouv.fr](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/precisions-sur-le-service-de-verification-en-ligne), these are the two thirteen-digit identifiers to type into the tax notice verification service, which a landlord can use.
- **The 2D-Doc code.** According to impots.gouv.fr, the code printed on the tax notice is used to check that the notice has not been altered. Likewise, leave any verification QR code.
- **Your photo and your name**, on the identity document: the [decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493) asks for a document that carries its holder’s photograph.
- **Your income, your employer and the dates**: this is what serves to assess the file.

## Are you allowed to hide part of a document?

As far as we know, no law settles it. [Décret n° 2015-1437](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493) allows copies, and says the originals must be available if the landlord asks for them. Our advice: hide little, say that you did, and keep your originals.

The [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-11/logement_social-fiche04.pdf), the French data protection authority, has commented on a neighbouring case, which is not that of a rental applicant. About the payslips of their own staff that social landlords pass on to justify service charges, it notes that the social security number or the address are in principle not needed, and that sharing them can breach the principle of data minimisation unless all the irrelevant items are redacted.

## Why is a black box not enough?

Because a box drawn in a PDF editor can leave the text underneath, where it can still be copied. Blur and pixelation can sometimes be reversed. These are general technical observations, which the pages cited here do not cover. When in doubt, check: a reliable mask removes the information from the file. See [how to redact a PDF on a Mac](https://monsieurdossier.com/en/guides/redact-pdf-mac-without-uploading.html).

## How do you check that a mask holds?

1. Open the redacted copy.
2. Try to select the text under the mask, then to copy it.
3. Search for the hidden number with the search function.
4. If the text can be copied or found, the mask removed nothing: start again with another tool.

## What if the landlord refuses the redacted copy?

Show the original at the viewing or at signing: the [decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493) says originals can be asked for. You can also go through DossierFacile, the government service: according to the [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/location-dun-bien-immobilier-quels-justificatifs), it lets the tenant upload the authorised documents and pass them on to the landlord.
