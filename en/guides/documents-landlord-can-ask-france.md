# What documents can a landlord ask for when you rent in France?

> To rent a home in France, a landlord or agency can ask for four kinds of supporting documents: one identity document, one proof of your current address, one or more documents about your occupation, and one or more documents about your income. The list is set by [décret n° 2015-1437](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493) of 5 November 2015. A landlord cannot require a document that is not on it: according to [service-public.gouv.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F1169), the French government’s information site, a landlord who does so risks a fine.

## Which rentals does this list apply to?

It applies to homes rented as a main residence, furnished or unfurnished, and to the short “bail mobilité” lease: the [law of 6 July 1989](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000509310/) extends the rule to all three. The [decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493) states that it does not apply to applications for social housing.

## Which identity document can a landlord ask for?

One valid identity document that carries your photo. [Annex I of the decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493) gives the list:

- a national identity card, French or foreign
- a passport, French or foreign
- a driving licence, French or foreign
- a document proving your right to stay in France, such as a temporary residence permit, a resident card or the card of a citizen of the European Union or the European Economic Area

## Which proof of address can a landlord ask for?

One proof of your current address, from those listed in the [decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493):

- your last three rent receipts, or failing that a statement from your previous landlord, or the landlord’s agent, that your rent and charges are paid up
- a certificate of domiciliation from an approved organisation
- a signed statement from the person who hosts you
- your latest property tax notice, or failing that the title deed of your main home

> The tenant’s list does not include an electricity, gas or water bill. Those bills are on the guarantor’s list.

## Which documents about your occupation can a landlord ask for?

One or more documents, depending on your situation. The list is the [decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493)’s:

- **Employee or intern.** The employment or internship contract, or failing that a letter from the employer stating the job, the proposed pay, the start date and, if there is one, the length of the trial period.
- **Student.** A student card or a certificate of enrolment for the current year.
- **Trading company.** A K or K bis extract from the company register, less than three months old.
- **Craft business.** The registration record in the Registre national des entreprises, less than three months old.
- **Self-employed.** A copy of the INSEE identification certificate.
- **Independent professional (profession libérale).** A copy of the professional card.
- **Other professionals.** Any recent document that proves the activity.

## Which documents about your income can a landlord ask for?

One or more documents, from those listed in the [decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493):

- your latest or second-latest tax notice, or notice of non-taxation. If all or part of your income was taxed in another country, the tax notice of that country or an equivalent document from its tax authority
- your last three payslips
- proof of payment of an internship allowance
- for the self-employed, the last two balance sheets, or failing that a statement of income for the current year drawn up by an accountant
- proof of payment over the last three months of benefits, pensions and allowances, or proof that the entitlement has been opened, issued by the paying body
- a housing benefit simulation, drawn up by the paying body or by you
- the grant award notice, for a student on a grant
- the title deed of a property, or the latest property tax notice
- proof of rental income, life annuities or investment income

## Do you have to hand over originals?

No. [Article 1 of the decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493) says the documents can be copies of the originals. They are written in French or translated into French, and amounts are converted into euros. The originals must be available if the landlord asks to see them.

## Which documents can a landlord ask the guarantor for?

The [decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000031444493) sets a second list, in annex II, for the person who acts as guarantor: one identity document, one proof of address, and documents about occupation and income. For the address, the guarantor’s list is different: latest rent receipt, a water, gas or electricity bill less than three months old, a home insurance certificate less than three months old, the latest property tax notice or a title deed.

If the guarantor is a company or an organisation, the decree provides for a K bis extract less than three months old, the articles of association or another document proving that it legally exists, and proof of identity for its representative.

## What does a landlord risk by asking for anything else?

According to [service-public.gouv.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F1169), a landlord or agency that demands a document outside the list risks a fine of up to €3,000, or €15,000 for a legal entity such as a company. [Article 22-2 of the law](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000509310/) states that the fine is imposed by the prefect and that its amount depends on how serious the facts are.

The same article also bars a landlord from requiring an ascendant or a descendant of the applicant, a parent or a child for example, to co-sign. See [the documents a French landlord is not allowed to ask for](https://monsieurdossier.com/en/guides/documents-landlord-cannot-ask-france.html).
